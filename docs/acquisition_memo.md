# Acquisition memo — Vaultline Archive Ltd

*Synthetic data. Prepared from `keel_saas_model.xlsx` (tabs `MA_*`). GBP. The deal is assumed to close at the model's opening date. Every Vaultline figure and every synergy is an illustrative assumption, not a finding.*

## Recommendation

**Proceed only if (a) the price is at or below about 4.0x ARR (£24m), or part of it is deferred against customer retention, and (b) funding headroom is increased before signing.** At the modelled 4.5x (£27.0m) the deal creates value in the Base and Upside cases but fails in the Downside, and it leaves too little cash to survive that case.

| | Base | Upside | Downside |
|---|---|---|---|
| Value created (NPV, £m) | **+7.3** | +16.8 | **−12.6** |
| Pro-forma ARR, month 24 (£m) | 76.7 | 89.9 | 56.5 |
| Pro-forma lowest month-end cash (£m) | 6.0 | 9.8 | **−6.2** |

The Downside lowers Vaultline's growth, doubles its churn, and cuts synergies to 40% of plan, alongside Northgate's own Downside. Pro-forma cash goes negative in that case, so the model's master check turns red there by design.

## The deal in numbers (Base)

- **Price:** 4.5x ARR = £27.0m enterprise value, plus £3.5m of cash acquired = £30.5m equity price. That is 4.2x revenue and 17x EBITDA for a business growing 15% a year, fading to 4% by year 5.
- **Standalone value:** the DCF gives £21.0m (range £18.1m–£25.1m), so we pay a **£6.0m premium** for control. Comps (synthetic, EV/ARR 3.9x–7.2x) put the range at £23m–£43m, but Vaultline grows more slowly than most peers, so the lower half is the fair reference.
- **Funding:** £15.0m term loan at 8% plus £15.5m of Northgate cash. Northgate's cash falls from £24.0m to a low of £6.0m, and the loan is a bullet with no repayment inside the 24 months.
- **Synergies:** £1.2m a year of cost savings and cross-sell of Vaultline's product to Northgate's base (0.8 sales a month at £45k, at 65% contribution margin), starting in month 7. Present value at full plan: £15.9m. Integration costs: £3.0m over 12 months.
- **Break-even:** the deal needs **54% of planned synergies** to break even at 4.5x. At 4.0x it needs 35%; at 5.5x it needs 92% (`Sensitivity`, grid 3).
- **Payback:** more than 5 years on cash flow (£12.8m recovered against £27.5m). The value is in the terminal value, which is 68% of Vaultline's DCF.
- **Growth and quality:** pro-forma ARR is 12% higher at month 24 and the Rule of 40 rises from 27% to 31%. But ARR growth *falls* (31% against 33% standalone at month 12), because Vaultline grows more slowly than Northgate.

## Key risks

1. **Retention after a change of control.** Vaultline's churn is the largest swing factor. The Downside doubles it to 12%.
2. **Cash headroom.** In the Downside the pro-forma business is short of cash by at least £6.2m within 24 months. Annual-upfront billing hides this in Base.
3. **Synergy delivery.** Over half of the planned synergies are needed just to break even, and the cross-sell volume is unproven.
4. **Integration cost overrun.** Integration costs are not scaled with synergies in the model; they are a fixed £3.0m.
5. **Impairment.** Goodwill (£19.2m) and acquired intangibles (£11.3m) together make up essentially the whole £30.5m price, because Vaultline has almost no tangible net assets. Any underperformance shows up as a write-down.

## What diligence would need to confirm (deal-breakers)

1. **ARR quality:** that £6.0m is contracted, recurring and not inflated by multi-year or one-off items, and that it ties to deferred revenue and billings.
2. **Change-of-control clauses and concentration:** that the top customers cannot terminate or reprice on acquisition.
3. **Churn history by cohort:** the 6% assumption against actuals, and why customers leave.
4. **Cross-sell evidence:** named Northgate customers who have asked for archiving, and Vaultline's technical fit to Northgate's platform.
5. **Regulatory and security posture:** the product holds regulated records, so audit findings, retention rules and breach history matter.
6. **Cost base:** that the 25% EBITDA margin is sustainable and includes the true cost of the intercompany contract and any founder under-payment.
7. **Funding:** committed financing that covers the Downside, such as a revolving facility or a larger loan, before signing.

## Model limitations

- Closing is at t=0. Locked-box vs completion accounts, earn-outs and working-capital adjustments are not modelled.
- Synergy cash is assumed collected in the month earned, and synergy revenue has no churn.
- The purchase price allocation uses percentages of EV for intangibles. In practice a valuation specialist sets them.
- Cash taxes are not reduced by the deal's losses or amortisation (no group relief, no tax-deductible goodwill).
- Years 3–5 for Vaultline are a fade assumption, not a forecast.
